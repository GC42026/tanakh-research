What makes an Exodus story distinctive?

Start with something Tanakh says about other peoples’ journeys.

Explore this study in the atlas

Tanakh supplies its own comparison

Amos puts Israel’s departure from Egypt beside the departures of the Philistines from Caphtor and the Arameans from Kir. YHVH speaks as the agent in all three. That gives us a comparison inside Tanakh itself. The movement of a people can be shared across stories. The interpretation of that movement and the obligations drawn from it can differ. Amos 9:7

Devarim connects the memory of slavery to obligations toward the stranger, orphan, and widow. It gives the memory a concrete form in what a landholder leaves at harvest. That is a more precise candidate for comparison than liberation in general: remembered vulnerability is made the reason for a continuing limit on what the prosperous may keep. Devarim 24:17–22

How to test a claim of uniqueness

Define the unit of comparison first. An image, a plot sequence, an ethical obligation, and a theological claim can have different distributions. Date each witness and ask what literary contact was possible. A close parallel may be inherited or independently developed. A claim to uniqueness needs a stated comparison set and serious attention to counterexamples. This study begins with the Exodus and leaves the wider verdict open.
