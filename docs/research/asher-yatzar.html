Where did Asher Yatzar come from?

A blessing about the body, and a Talmudic discussion that preserves its shape.

The witness in Berakhot

Berakhot 60b gives a blessing said after leaving the privy. It speaks of a human being formed with wisdom, with openings and hollow spaces whose proper function makes life possible. The same discussion preserves alternative endings. Rav gives a formula about healing the sick. Shmuel objects and proposes healing all flesh. Rav Sheshet proposes doing wonders. Rav Pappa joins the last two formulations. The passage gives us the wording and the discussion of its close. The composer of the opening remains unknown. Berakhot 60b

The blessing puts the wonder in ordinary bodily function. In the vocabulary of this project, it invites a question about the microcosm. What does the act of giving thanks disclose about a body whose life depends on the ordering of its parts? Genesis 2:7 and Psalm 139 provide biblical resonances for that question. Their relation to the wording of the blessing is a subject for study. Bereishit 2:7 · Tehillim 139:13–16

What an earlier witness could tell us

A manuscript comparison could show where the wording remained stable and where rites diverged. It would also let us ask whether the early siddurim transmit a formula already substantially settled in the Bavli, or preserve alternatives that the printed text obscures.
