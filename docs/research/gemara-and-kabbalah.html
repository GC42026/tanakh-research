What place should Kabbalah have in Torah study?

Rambam’s curriculum and Baal HaSulam’s argument for studying the inner Torah.

What does advanced study include?

Rambam divides a beginner’s study among written Torah, oral Torah, and Talmud, his term here for reasoning from what has been learned. He includes the subjects called Pardes in the third division. In this passage his term refers to esoteric subjects discussed in the opening laws of the Mishneh Torah. That makes it important to ask what “advanced study” included for a particular author before judging a later curriculum by the same vocabulary. Rambam, Laws of Torah Study 1:11–12 · Laws of the Foundations of the Torah 4:13

What does study do to the person studying?

Baal HaSulam describes Torah itself as having inward and outward dimensions. In his introduction to the Zohar, the weight a person gives the inward dimension has consequences at the level of the individual and the wider world. That is a Kabbalistic account of tikkun and a reason to study Kabbalah. To evaluate how a particular yeshiva trains students, we would need its actual curriculum, the role of practice and formation in it, and evidence of what teachers intend. Baal HaSulam, Introduction to Zohar §§66–71

The question for this journal is what becomes available when the same passage is studied for its practical obligations and for the changes it asks of desire and intention. The reading of “Come to Pharaoh” begins with a word and follows it into a question about what governs the reader.
