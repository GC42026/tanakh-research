Sefirot, gematria, and computation

What can be counted, what can be compared, and what still needs a test.

Words in Tanakh and concepts in Kabbalah

I Divrei HaYamim 29:11 clusters greatness, might, splendor, triumph, and majesty in a single prayer. Yeshayahu 11:2 brings wisdom and understanding together with might. Esther uses keter for a royal crown. These passages are good entry points for asking which words later became terms of art and how their earlier meanings bear on that development. A reproducible count must specify a Hebrew edition, how prefixes and inflections are handled, and which matches are lexical or interpretive. I Divrei HaYamim 29:11 · Yeshayahu 11:2 · Esther 2:17

What a numerical comparison can establish

Sefer Yetzirah opens with thirty-two paths and then speaks of ten sefirot and twenty-two letters. In standard gematria the spellings יהוה and אלהים give 26 and 86. Each total is a sum of letter values. The sum is insensitive to order, so two differently ordered texts can give the same number while saying different things. That matters when comparing gematria to a language model, where order and context can affect a token’s representation. Sefer Yetzirah 1:1–2 · Vaswani et al., Attention Is All You Need

The theological question is what it means for created computation to depend on YHVH Elohim. A computational question is whether a specified gematria rule predicts anything about a defined model or corpus. For the latter, fix the rule and comparison before looking at the results, and report misses as well as matches. The two questions can inform one another while asking for different kinds of evidence.
