What connects Esav and Mitzrayim?

Why does Moshe bring up Egypt when asking Edom for passage?

The appeal to a brother

In Bamidbar 20:14–21, Moshe addresses Edom with כֹּה אָמַר אָחִיךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל (“Thus says your brother Israel”). He recounts the descent into Mitzrayim, the suffering there, and the departure. Edom refuses Israel passage and comes out in force. Bamidbar 20:14–21

Rashi’s reading of the debt

Bereishit says the brothers’ possessions were too great for them to dwell together. Esav left מִפְּנֵי יַעֲקֹב אָחִיו (“on account of Yaakov his brother”) and settled in Seir. Rashi draws on midrash to read a further motive. The exile foretold to Avraham was a debt that fell on both brothers. Esav departed and left Yaakov to bear it. Bereishit 36:6–8 · Bereishit 15:13 · Rashi on Bamidbar 20:14

Read this way, Moshe’s words carry an implied appeal. We bore what both of us owed. Will you let us pass?

Two other links

Esav married Machalat, daughter of Yishmael and granddaughter of Hagar the Egyptian. Yishmael also took a wife from Mitzrayim. The Torah leaves Machalat’s mother unnamed. Bereishit 28:9 · 16:1 · 21:21

Devarim pairs Edom and Mitzrayim in one verse. Israel is told not to abhor the Edomite because he is a brother, and not to abhor the Egyptian because Israel once lived as a stranger in his land. Devarim 23:8

An open question

What does brotherhood demand when one brother has carried a burden the two once shared?
