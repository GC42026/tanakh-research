What lies beneath the story?

The Zohar, the language of branches, and a reading of “Come to Pharaoh.”

Creation, sanctuary, person

Midrash Tanchuma relates the Mishkan to both the world and the human embryo. The Zohar offers another set of correspondences. In Beha’alotekha, Torah’s stories are the garment, its mitzvot the body. It then speaks of a soul and a soul of the soul, and traces a corresponding order above. In Baal HaSulam’s language of branches, the terms taken from this world name spiritual roots and their relations. His preface to the Zohar asks what those relations disclose to souls. This is the sense in which the Zohar can illuminate the inner and upper worlds. Tanchuma, Pekudei 3:3 · Zohar III 152a, Beha’alotekha · The Essence of the Wisdom of Kabbalah · Preface to Zohar §5

Why harden the heart?

In Shemot, YHVH sends Moshe and says He has hardened Pharaoh’s heart. The Zohar takes Moshe through successive chambers toward the great tannin, the power behind Egypt’s channels. Rabash hears בֹּא, “come,” as an invitation to go together. In his inward reading, the person discovers how thoroughly self-love governs desire. The discovery gives rise to a need that becomes a kli, a vessel for the help he calls neshamah. The hardening itself helps reveal the need. Shemot 10:1–2 · Zohar II 34a · Rabash, Come unto Pharaoh – 2

Pharaoh says he does not know YHVH. Yitro, the Midianite priest, says that now he knows. The Zohar places the giving of Torah in its fullness after that acknowledgment. This complicates any equation of the inner nations with Pharaoh. In the reading proposed here, the capacity once directed toward other powers can itself come to recognize YHVH. Baal HaSulam speaks explicitly of inner Israel and the inner nations within a person of Israel. The project asks how that distinction might work in every person. Shemot 5:2 · Shemot 18:11 · Zohar II 67b, Yitro · Introduction to Zohar §§66–67

Speaking within speaking

PaRDeS gives us peshat, remez, derash and sod. We use “speaking within speaking” as a working name for a further question. What would it mean to hear the divine address from inside the condition the verse describes? A reader can explain Rabash’s account of a vessel. A person undergoing it would have to discover what makes that vessel necessary. The Zohar’s “soul of the soul” is a parallel under study, and the provenance of this fivefold formulation remains open.
