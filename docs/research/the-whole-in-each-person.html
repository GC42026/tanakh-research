The whole in each person

If one soul is present whole in each of us, what exactly is it that feels separate?

Explore this study in the atlas

The soul and what it can receive

Baal HaSulam opens 600,000 Souls with the breath of life in Bereishit 2:7. Spirit, he argues, cannot be cut into pieces. The one soul of Adam HaRishon is therefore present whole in each of the children of Israel. Yet the person may experience only a spark, or experience their own soul as a single organ and take that organ for the whole self. The distinction is between the soul’s wholeness and the degree to which that wholeness is disclosed. Bereishit 2:7 · Baal HaSulam, 600,000 Souls

The sign of fuller disclosure is relational. A person senses their soul in the others and recognizes their interdependence. Baal HaSulam says a deficiency in one affects what the others receive, and someone who attains more prays on behalf of the generation. That suggests a stronger meaning of microcosm. The person beside me can disclose something of the common soul that I cannot receive in isolation. 600,000 Souls

Who came out of Egypt?

Shemot counts about 600,000 men on foot at the departure. A chapter later it puts the telling in the singular: what YHVH did for me when I came out of Egypt. The Mishnah makes that first-person identification a task for every generation. Baal HaSulam makes a further claim. The journeys undergone by the whole are to be undergone by the individual soul. He speaks of Moshe at the Exodus and the giving of Torah, Yehoshua at the conquest, and Shelomo at the building of the Temple. In this reading the names mark different disclosures of the one soul. Shemot 12:37 · Shemot 13:8 · Mishnah Pesachim 10:5 · 600,000 Souls

The question then is what the word I includes when I say I came out of Egypt. Baal HaSulam explicitly describes the full presence of the soul within Israel. Whether and how the same account extends to every human being remains open here.
