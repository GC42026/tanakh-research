Method & sources

This first atlas follows images of heaven and earth from Yaakov’s dream to Bavel, Sinai, and the Mishkan. It includes Torah passages, a person, parashot, motifs, and rabbinic readings. The source pane gives the reference, a Hebrew excerpt when available, the English sense, and a link to Sefaria.

Reading beyond time and space
In the reading taught by Michael Laitman and Bnei Baruch, names from Olam HaZeh point to spiritual roots. Set aside physical time and space and trace relationships between spiritual objects and changes of state. The 3D layout helps you navigate the sources. Read the project’s guiding principle.
Macro
The overview of the current collection. Journal studies reveal their citations in Micro view. Sefaria displays connections in batches.
Micro
The selected node and its direct neighbors. Following a link brings the new node’s neighborhood into view.
Relationships
Linked nodes attract and all nodes repel. Direct and shared sources use the shortest, strongest springs, followed by context links and interpretive comparisons. The whole graph affects each distance. Heights, directions, and node sizes carry no rank or spiritual value. In Shapes, the sphere, octahedron, and helix arrange the same nodes without the relationship forces.
Gematria
Standard values (mispar hechrechi), with final letters valued like their ordinary forms. Vowel points, accents, punctuation, and spaces are ignored. Values apply to the specific Hebrew spelling shown. Numeric equality can be the basis of a derash, as with the ladder and Sinai.
Bnei Baruch readings
Readings appear with author and locator. A comparison in the atlas can place two texts beside each other even when the lineage source does not discuss the passage directly.
Relationship labels
Text and narrative links come from the passages. Traditional links point to a named Jewish source. Thematic links represent a reading proposed by this atlas. Journal citations show where an article cites a source. In the Sefaria reader, relationship labels and source anchors come from Sefaria’s Links API.
